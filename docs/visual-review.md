# What to look at when judging a piece

| Question | What to check in the video |
|---|---|
| Where should the viewer look now? | One perceptible focus; text, camera and particles never compete for the same attention. |
| What does the movement explain? | Moving, separating or transforming something reveals a relationship, a change or a consequence. |
| Do we recognise the same object between scenes? | Shape, scale, colour, position and trajectory keep enough continuity. |
| Does the material belong to the same piece? | Palette, type, stroke weight, texture and resolution follow one rule. |
| Is there time to understand it? | Reading time is checked on the target device, not derived from the BPM. |
| Do actions have weight and intent? | Anticipation, acceleration, impact and recovery fit the object and the tone. |
| Do voice, music and effects work together? | Words are intelligible and sound accents land on relevant actions. |
| Is the content true to the product? | Every screen, relationship and result shown can be defended. |

A technically correct render can still be confusing or monotonous. Record observable defects and fix them in the final file. A beautiful video does not make a commercial or financial claim true.

## Frame review routine

1. Render stills at each scene change, each important gesture and a few frames around every transition (`render.mjs <piece> still t1,t2,…`).
2. Open them one by one, in order, at a size where text is readable. Do not judge from a grid.
3. For each frame ask: is the focus clear, is anything cut by the safe zone, does any text overlap another element, is anything frozen that should move?
4. Watch the preview with sound at phone size before rendering the final.
